\section{Results}

We analysed \NRollingRuns\ rolling-horizon runs and \NBaselineRuns\ full-horizon baselines
(\NRuns\ runs in total; Table~\ref{tab:scenarios} describes the scenarios). Every run is scored on
its stitched plan, replayed against the full 112-day problem. The two largest simulated-annealing
runs (North Island and Prince George, 40 blocks, 112-day windows, 1-day lock) were still solving at
the time of writing and are excluded; the remaining \NRollingRuns\ rolling runs cover every other
configuration.

\subsection{Tractability and window outcomes}

The full-horizon baselines set the reference for each scenario (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}). They
are not uniformly good. Where the fleet can harvest all of the scheduled volume within 112 days,
the baseline reaches 100\% of the volume (Kamloops and Prince George at 6 and 18 blocks). Where it
cannot, or where the solver runs out of time, it delivers less: 54--68\% for North Island at every
size, and 59--92\% for the larger Kamloops and Prince George cases.

Solver behaviour explains much of this (Table~\ref{tab:windows}). The MIP reached its 1800~s
first-stage limit in 4.4\% of windows at 6 blocks, 37.9\% at 18 blocks, and 70.0\% at 40 blocks.
The simulated annealing never reached a limit, because it stops after a fixed number of iterations.
Across all MIP rolling windows, 9 returned no solution and 17 were empty, all at 18 and 40 blocks;
no simulated-annealing window failed. All \GurobiSolvesLogged\ MIP solves longer than 100~s ran at a
median of \GurobiWorkRateMedian\ work units per second (5th--95th percentile
\GurobiWorkRateLow--\GurobiWorkRateHigh), about half the rate measured on an otherwise idle host.

\subsection{Delivered volume: planning horizon and re-optimization interval}

Delivered volume is close to the best plan found for each scenario in almost every configuration
(Fig.~\ref{fig:delivered}). Aggregated over the three study areas, the median run delivers
\RelBestMedianMIPLockOne\% (MIP) and \RelBestMedianSALockOne\% (SA) of the best known plan at a
1-day lock; \RelBestMedianMIPLockSeven\% and \RelBestMedianSALockSeven\% at 7 days; and
\RelBestMedianMIPLockFourteen\% and \RelBestMedianSALockFourteen\% at 14 days. The worst runs reach
\RelBestMinMIPLockOne\% (MIP) and \RelBestMinSALockOne\% (SA) at a 1-day lock, and
\RelBestMinMIPLockFourteen\% and \RelBestMinSALockFourteen\% at 14 days.

The differences between configurations are small. At 40 blocks, where the effects are largest, the
median relative volume by window length and lock span is:

Two patterns stand out. First, window length matters little: the 4- and 8-week windows are the best
or tied-best in every lock span, and the 16-week windows are no better and are sometimes a little
worse. The 16-week penalty coincides with the point at which MIP windows most often hit the time
limit, so it is at least partly a tractability effect rather than a planning one. Second, the
re-optimization interval matters only at its longest value: the 1-day and 7-day locks are
indistinguishable, and the 14-day lock is 1--3 percentage points lower at 40 blocks.

\subsection{Feasibility and continuity of the implemented schedule}

Feasibility of the replayed plans differs by method. All \NRollingRuns\ simulated-annealing plans
replay with no sequencing violations. \NRunsWithViolations\ of the 117 MIP rolling runs show raw
violations (58 in total), but only \NRunsWithMaterialViolations\ run has any beyond a
\TinyThreshold~m$^3$ tolerance: the rest are negligible-production rows that FHOPS replays as
violations (UBC-FRESH/fhops\#157). Feasibility is therefore not a discriminating factor between
configurations; it is a property of the solver.

Continuity is descriptive here, because machine moves are free in these scenarios and neither
method is rewarded for keeping a machine on a block. Median continuity is high
at 6 blocks (about 0.72 for both methods) and falls with size, more so for MIP (0.36 at 18 blocks,
0.20 at 40 blocks) than for SA (0.42 and 0.28). Machine moves rise with size and are slightly
higher for MIP at 40 blocks. These are incidental properties of the schedules, not optimized ones.

\subsection{Problem size, solution method, and computing effort}

Problem size has the clearest effect (Fig.~\ref{fig:effort}). At 6 blocks both methods reach the
best known plan in every configuration. At 18 blocks the median MIP run reaches 100\% and the
median SA run 99.0\%. At 40 blocks the median MIP run reaches 100\% and the median SA run 97.8\%.

Measured against the MIP run of the same configuration, SA delivers more volume in 8 of 106 pairs,
all at 40 blocks with 14-day or 112-day windows, where it reaches up to 1.11 times the MIP volume;
in the remaining pairs MIP is at least as good. The MIP advantage comes at a cost: median wall time
at 40 blocks is \WallMedianHoursMIPForty\ h for MIP against \WallMedianHoursSAForty\ h for SA, and
at 18 blocks \WallMedianHoursMIPEighteen\ h against \WallMedianHoursSAEighteen\ h. The weak MIP
cells are the ones with no-solution or empty windows; where the MIP found a plan, it was at least
as good as SA.
